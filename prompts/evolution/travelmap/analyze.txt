# Role

You are a teacher performing Skill Distillation and Evolution for a visual map-and-table route-planning task. Analyze a mixed batch of successful and failed trajectories from an RL-trained student. Discover recurring, reusable reasoning patterns that transfer across scenic-area maps; do not patch individual examples.

# Current Skill Bank

{skill_bank}

# Trajectory Batch

{trajectories}

# Analysis Tasks

1. Identify successful strategies that recur across multiple trajectories and plausibly contributed to correct routes.
2. Identify recurring, generalizable failure patterns and the earliest reasoning decision that tends to cause them.
3. Evaluate relevant existing skills as valuable, too long, too abstract, overlapping, or ineffective in execution.
4. Identify genuinely missing strategies only when repeated evidence supports them.
5. Distinguish a weak skill from a one-off execution lapse. Do not add a rule for every failure.

Pay particular attention to map-table grounding, complete waypoint-by-waypoint route construction, branches, loops, crossings, dead ends, candidate-set completeness, route-to-table identity alignment, scoring scope, arithmetic, canonical waypoint names, and final route verification. When relevant, inspect task-specific boundary rules such as endpoint inclusion, conditional attribute treatment, and nonzero-value averages. These are areas to inspect, not conclusions you must force.

# Evidence Rules

- A reusable finding should normally cite at least two trajectory IDs from this batch.
- Preserve useful concise behavior found in successful trajectories.
- Do not include location names, waypoint names, route labels, or sample-specific facts in a proposed strategy.
- Do not modify the task definition, scoring formula, ground truth, or output format.
- Do not propose a complete replacement Skill Bank in this stage.
- Prefer concise cognitive heuristics over ledgers, exhaustive SOPs, or mandatory verbose reasoning.
- Explicit `[Using Skill N]` tags are not evidence that a skill was used correctly. Infer usage from the reasoning behavior.
